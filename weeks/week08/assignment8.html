Part B — Written (12 points, 3 each)

1. (3 points) Your MAR is a MEM_8BIT, not a REGISTER_8BIT. Why does it not need an enable or output buffers?

2. (3 points) Why does the RAM use two 4×16 decoders instead of one 8×256 decoder? Compare the two designs — and include the gate every byte needs that the decoders do not provide.

3. (3 points) You load 01000000 into the MAR and raise e. What does o show, and why is that not 00000000? What happens if you pulse s at that address instead?

4. (3 points) $t0 = 0x10010000 and a program runs lw $t1, 2($t0). What does MARS do, and why does lw need its address to be a multiple of 4?

Part C — Address arithmetic (18 points, 3 each)

Give addresses in hex, and other numbers as each question asks. Show enough working that a wrong answer can earn partial credit.

1. (3 points) $s0 = 0x10010008. Give the effective address of each instruction.

2. (3 points) A C array int a[10] starts at 0x10010040.

3. (3 points) The index i is in $t0 and the base of a is in $s0. Write the two instructions that leave the address of a[i] in $t1.

4. (3 points) For each RAM address, give the row, the column, the byte number, and whether your RAM has that byte.

5. (3 points) A byte in memory holds 0xC8. What does each instruction leave in the register, in hex and in decimal?

6. (3 points) MARS stores .word 0x0A0B0C0D at address 0x10010004. Which byte is at each address?

Part D — A MARS array program (20 points)

Submit as lastname_firstname_week8.asm. This is a separate file upload — do not paste it into the PDF. Start from this data section, unchanged:

.data vals: .word 14, -3, 27, 8, -11, 30, 5, 2 len: .word 8 out: .space 32

Then, in .text:

  1. Sum the elements of vals into $s0.
  2. Find the largest element and leave it in $s1. The comparison must be signed — one element is negative.
  3. Copy vals into out in reverse order, so out[0] is the last element of vals, one lw and one sw per element.
Requirements
  • .data first, then .text, with no main: label and no .globl main — the course’s standing layout.
  • Every loop takes its bound from len. Do not type 8 into a branch.
  • Index walk or pointer walk, as you prefer — page 4 shows both.
  • Pseudo-instructions (la, li, move, blt and the rest) are permitted.
  • Nothing here needs multiplication or division. mult, div, mfhi, mflo and lui are not taught and must not appear in your source — multiply by 4 with sll. (MARS’s Basic column will show lui where it expands your la; that is the assembler’s, not yours.)
  • Comment every line that is not obvious.
