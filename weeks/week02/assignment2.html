What this assignment is for

Two things. First, it checks that you can actually use the logic from this week — truth tables, Boolean algebra, and the NAND derivations — rather than just recognize it.

Second, and less obviously: this is where your Logisim habits get set. The single-file discipline you establish here is the one you will still be using in Week 14 when that file contains a working CPU. It is much easier to start correctly than to reorganize later.

Quiz 1 in Week 4 covers Weeks 1–4, and its problems look very much like Parts A through D. Treat this as quiz preparation, not just as points.

Boolean Notation: Read This Before Part B

Parts B and C live or die on reading these symbols correctly. Two minutes here saves an hour later.

Written asMeansAlso written
ABA AND BA · B, A * B
A + BA OR BA | B
A'NOT A¬A, !A, Ā
A ⊕ BA XOR BA ^ B
The prime applies to ONE variable — read it like an exponent

In A'B, the prime applies only to the variable it immediately follows.

  • A'B = (NOT A) AND B
  • AB' = A AND (NOT B)
  • A'B' = (NOT A) AND (NOT B)

To negate an entire expression, parenthesize it first: (AB)' means NOT (A AND B).

And these are not the same thing. Try A = 1, B = 0: A'B' = 0 · 1 = 0, while (AB)' = (1·0)' = 0' = 1. The relationship between them is De Morgan's law — which is the entire subject of Part C.

Also: + is OR, not addition. In Boolean algebra 1 + 1 = 1. There is one bit of output; it is either on or it is not.

Part E — Logisim Submission 20 points

17. (20 pts) Build all five circuits below as separate tabs in your single CSC210_YourName.circ file.

Rules for every circuit in this part
  • Primitive gates only — AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR, XNOR, plus input and output pins. No Adder, Multiplexer, Decoder, or anything else from the other library panels.
  • Label every pin. Unlabeled pins cost points and make your circuit hard to grade.
  • Test every input combination with the poke tool before you submit. Two-input circuits have 4; three-input circuits have 8.
  • A wire showing blue means undefined — something is unconnected. Trace it back and fix it.

Circuit 1 — NAND_NOT (2 pts)

A NOT gate built from one NAND gate.

  • Place one NAND gate and one input pin. This circuit has a single input — do not place a second pin; an unconnected pin floats and Logisim will flag it.
  • Wire the input pin to both inputs of the NAND gate.
  • Label the input A and the output NOT A.

Verify: A=0 → output 1; A=1 → output 0.

Circuit 2 — NAND_AND (3 pts)

An AND gate built from two NAND gates.

  • Wire inputs A and B into the first NAND.
  • Wire that NAND's output into both inputs of the second NAND, which acts as a NOT.
  • Label pins A, B, and A AND B.

Verify against the AND truth table: output is 1 only for A=1, B=1.

Circuit 3 — NAND_OR (3 pts)

An OR gate built from three NAND gates.

  • NAND 1: both inputs tied to A, producing A'.
  • NAND 2: both inputs tied to B, producing B'.
  • NAND 3: fed by the outputs of NAND 1 and NAND 2.
  • Label pins A, B, and A OR B.

Verify against the OR truth table: output is 0 only for A=0, B=0.

Circuit 4 — BOOLEAN_EXPR (3 pts)

Implement F = (A AND B) OR (NOT C) using primitive gates.

  • Three input pins A, B, C; one output pin F.
  • Decompose it: one AND for AB, one NOT for C', one OR to combine them. Three gates.

Circuit 5 — DEMORGAN_CHECK (4 pts)

Prove De Morgan's first law — (AB)' = A' + B' — by building both sides in one circuit and letting the circuit itself confirm they agree.

  • Two input pins A and B. Both halves share these same two pins — that is what makes the comparison meaningful.
  • Upper half: AND gate on A and B, then a NOT. Output pin LEFT_SIDE.
  • Lower half: NOT on A, NOT on B, then an OR. Output pin RIGHT_SIDE.
  • Align the two output pins vertically so you can read them together.
  • Comparison harness: feed LEFT_SIDE and RIGHT_SIDE into an XOR gate. Its output goes to a third pin, EQUAL.
Why the XOR is the clever part

XOR outputs 1 when its inputs differ. So EQUAL reads 0 whenever the two sides agree, and jumps to 1 the instant they disagree.

Toggle all four input combinations. If EQUAL never leaves 0, you have demonstrated De Morgan's law in hardware rather than just asserted it on paper. And if you miswire something, EQUAL goes high and tells you immediately — the circuit grades itself.

Seven gates total: 1 AND, 3 NOT, 1 OR, 1 XOR.

Optional challenge — no points, some insight

Rebuild Circuit 4 using only NAND gates. How many do you need?

You will find it takes considerably more than the three mixed gates. That is exactly why real chips use several gate types even though NAND alone is provably sufficient: universality is about what is possible, not about what is efficient.

Getting Help

Before you get stuck for an hour
  • Conceptual trouble in Parts B or C? Re-read the notation guide above. In my experience most Part B errors are precedence errors, not algebra errors.
  • Circuit gives the wrong answer? Build the truth table first, then find the first row where the circuit disagrees. That row tells you which gate to look at.
  • Blue wire in Logisim? Something is unconnected. Blue means undefined, not zero.
  • Tool trouble? Week 2 page 4 has the nine-minute Logisim walkthrough, and the official documentation is thorough.
  • Still stuck? Ask — during class, office hours, or the course discussion. Getting unstuck on Week 2 matters more than usual, because everything after this builds on it.
Academic integrity

Discuss concepts with classmates freely — that is how people learn this material. The work you submit must be your own: your own circuits, your own algebra, your own explanations.

Submitting a circuit you cannot explain is worth nothing to you in Week 4, when Quiz 1 asks you to do it again alone.
