Part 2 — RACE_BENCH ~30 minTogether

Six MEM_1BIT cells in a row, each one’s output feeding the next one’s input. The odd cells share one set line, s1; the even cells share another, s2. That is half of your stepper’s chain with everything else taken away.

  1. Place six MEM_1BIT instances in a row and label them Q1 to Q6 with text objects. Wire each one’s Q to the next one’s In.
  2. An input pin d into Q1’s In.
  3. An input pin s1 to the Set of Q1, Q3 and Q5; an input pin s2 to the Set of Q2, Q4 and Q6.
  4. Output pins q1 to q6, one on each cell. Every input pin pull-down.
Empty the chain first — one minute
  1. A fresh bench shows E on all six outputs. Correct, as in Week 4: no cell has been written.
  2. d = 0. Raise s1 and s2, then drop both. All six read 0.

Write down what you just did. You will need it again — and in Q2 you will explain why it worked.

2
Walk a 1 down the chain

Set d = 1 and leave it there. Now pulse one set line at a time — s1 up and down, then s2 up and down — and after each pulse record q1 to q6 on the sheet. Keep going until q6 is 1.

Count the pulses. How many did it take for the 1 to reach Q6? How many cells did the 1 advance per pulse?

Compare With the Room — before step 3

Empty the chain again. Then, with d = 1: if you raise s1 and s2 together and drop them together, how many of the six cells will hold 1? Everybody answers in the chat before anyone clicks, and we count.

3
The race

Do it: empty the chain, d = 1, raise s1 and s2 together, drop them together. Record all six outputs.

Empty the chain once more, and this time overlap the pulses: raise s1; raise s2 while s1 is still high; drop s1; drop s2. Record the six outputs. Was overlapping for a moment any safer than raising them together?

Open after you have recorded step 3: what you just saw

A cell whose Set is high is transparent: its output follows its input. When every cell is open at once, the 1 at d passes through Q1, which passes it to Q2, which passes it to Q3, and on to Q6, in one pulse — a race through a chain of open latches. The only thing that limits how far a value travels is a closed cell in its way. Two set lines that are never high together guarantee there is always one.

Your stepper drives its two set lines from clk and NOT clk, which can never both be 1. That is the whole reason it needs two cells per step — and it is the reason your registers are safe on a bus, too: the clock never lets a value pass through two open latches in one transfer.

Two questions — answer them on the observation sheet

Q1
Why twelve cells for six steps?

Your stepper has twelve cells. Using what step 3 showed you, say what would go wrong with a stepper of six cells all set by the same line, clk.

Q2
The one time a race is wanted

The stepper’s reset raises both set lines at once (page 2, §11.7: both ORs output 1). You did the same thing to empty this bench. Why is a race exactly right for a reset?

Part 3 — MICRO_ROM ~30 minYour own, then compare

Page 4 of this week stores your control unit’s table as words: one word per instruction class and step, one bit per control line. A memory chip would hold those words; Logisim’s ROM is in the Memory folder and not allowed. So you build the ROM the way the earliest computers did — a decoder picks one word line, and an OR gate on each output collects the word lines that have a 1 in that bit. It is called a diode matrix; here the OR gates play the diodes.

4
The address map

Eight addresses, eight words. Use this map; the words come from page 4’s ROM table, bits in the order regb_e tmp_s rega_e acc_s flags_s acc_e regb_s bus1:

addr holds word 000 ALU instruction, step 4 from page 4 001 ALU instruction, step 5 from page 4 010 ALU instruction, step 6 from page 4 011 CMP, step 6 from page 4 100 CLF, step 4 from page 4 101 (empty) 00000000 110 (empty) 00000000 111 (empty) 00000000

Copy the five words onto your sheet before you build. CMP’s steps 4 and 5 are the same as any ALU instruction’s, so in a real control store they would share addresses 000 and 001; only its step 6 differs. CLF’s steps 5 and 6 are empty.

  1. An input pin addr, 3 bits, into one DECODER_3TO8 instance.
  2. Eight output pins, 1 bit each, named regb_e, tmp_s, rega_e, acc_s, flags_s, acc_e, regb_s and bus1.
  3. For each output, look down its column of your word table. If one word has a 1 there, wire that decoder output straight to the pin. If two or more do, an OR gate collects them. If none does, a constant 0.
  4. Also combine the eight outputs through a splitter into an 8-bit output pin word, regb_e as its most significant bit, so a probe shows the whole word in the page’s order.
Verify all eight addresses

Step addr from 000 to 111 and record word each time. Every one must match your table. Count your gates: how many OR gates did the whole control table take?

Compare With the Room — before step 5

Here is an instruction your CPU does not have: INC RB, “RB ← RB + 1”. How many steps after fetch would it need, and which lines in each? Hint: BUS1 can make the ALU’s B input exactly 1, and an ALU result has to go through ACC. Everybody writes an answer before step 5.

5
Add an instruction by adding words

Work out INC RB’s words and put them at addresses 101 and 110 — two of the empty words. That means wiring decoder outputs 5 and 6 into the OR gates (or to the pins) of whichever lines your words need. Nothing else changes.

Verify all eight addresses again.

Open after you have written your INC words: one way to do it

Two steps. Step 4: RB drives the bus, so the ALU’s A input is RB; bus1 forces B to 1; the opcode is 000 outside step 5 of an ALU instruction, so the ALU adds; ACC captures RB + 1. That is regb_e, acc_s and bus1: 10010001. Step 5: ACC drives the bus and RB captures: acc_e and regb_s, 00000110 — the same word as an ALU instruction’s step 6. ACC is never enabled and set in the same step, so there is no loop.

Did you capture the flags too? That is a design choice, not a mistake: add flags_s to step 4 and INC sets Z when RB wraps from 255 to 0.

One more question — on the observation sheet

Q3
What would INC cost the hardwired control unit?

In MICRO_ROM, adding INC was two words. List what you would have to add to your Checkpoint 5 CONTROL_UNIT to give it INC: which new class line, which AND terms, and which existing OR gates change. Then say, in one sentence, which kind of control unit you would rather extend, and which one you would rather run fast.
