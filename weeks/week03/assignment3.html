Assignment 3: Combinational Circuits

100 points  |  Assesses: SLO1 — describe basic computer architecture, organization and design  |  Due: see the due date on this assignment in the course menu

Every circuit here is a component of your final project

This is not practice. The 8-bit adder you build becomes the arithmetic core of the ALU in Week 6. The decoders become the selection logic in the ALU, in RAM, and in the register file. You build them once, correctly, and then instantiate them for the rest of the semester.

That is why the pin names and widths below are exact, and why three of the 100 points are for matching them.

Before You Start

One file, all semester — no exceptions

Open the same CSC210_YourName.circ file you created in Week 2. The six circuits below are new tabs in that same file.

Do not create a new file. Do not create separate files and link them together — a "master" file that links to external circuits opens as a broken circuit on the grader's machine, and it is the single most common way students lose points in this course. Your Week 2 circuits stay exactly where they are; nothing gets deleted or replaced.

  • Logisim Classic 2.7.1 only — not Logisim Evolution.
  • Primitive gates only. NAND, AND, OR, NOT, NOR, XOR, XNOR, plus input and output pins. Logisim's built-in Adder, Decoder, Multiplexer, Register, RAM and ROM components are not permitted — building them from gates is the point of the exercise.
  • Verify each circuit against its truth table with the poke tool before moving on to the next one. A bug in FULL_ADDER becomes eight bugs in ADDER_8BIT.

2. (5 pts) Explain why a half adder cannot be used for any column of a multi-bit addition except the rightmost.

5. (5 pts) Reading these eight bits as unsigned values (0–255): the addition 10000001 + 10000001 in an 8-bit adder produces a sum of 00000010 and a carry-out of 1. Explain what the carry-out means, and why the 8-bit sum on its own is misleading.

Unsigned overflow — and what this question is not asking

This question is entirely about unsigned overflow. The true sum, 258, is larger than 255, and the carry-out is how the circuit tells you so.

Week 1's signed overflow is a different condition, detected by a different rule, and it is not what is being asked here.

If you noticed that these same bit patterns are −127 each in two's complement, you are reading ahead correctly — the same hardware supports both readings, and which one applies is a decision you make, not something the adder knows. Week 6 puts that decision inside the ALU. For this question, read them as unsigned.

Part C — Logisim Build 55 points

10. (55 pts) Build all six circuits as separate tabs in your single CSC210_YourName.circ file, matching the interfaces specified above.

Composition is the requirement, not just correct answers

ADDER_8BIT must be built from eight instances of your FULL_ADDER subcircuit. An 8-bit adder hand-wired from forty individual gates produces correct sums and has not met the requirement — it will be graded as such.

This is not pedantry. The point of the exercise is building a component once and reusing it, and a hand-wired version cannot be dropped into the ALU in Week 6 the way a composed one can.

Circuit 1 — HALF_ADDER

  1. New circuit tab named HALF_ADDER.
  2. Two 1-bit input pins, a and b.
  3. One XOR gate fed by both inputs → output pin sum.
  4. One AND gate fed by both inputs → output pin carry.
  5. Verify all four rows with the poke tool. Note the last row: 1 + 1 gives sum 0 and carry 1, which is binary 10 — and is exactly why a half adder is not enough on its own.

Circuit 2 — FULL_ADDER

  1. New circuit tab named FULL_ADDER. Three 1-bit inputs: a, b, cin.
  2. First stage: XOR of a and b gives the partial sum. AND of a and b gives the first carry.
  3. Second stage: XOR the partial sum with cin → output pin sum.
  4. AND the partial sum with cin to produce the second carry.
  5. OR the two carries → output pin cout. If either stage carried, the full adder carries.
  6. Five gates total: 2 XOR, 2 AND, 1 OR. Verify all eight input combinations.

Circuit 3 — ADDER_8BIT

  1. New circuit tab named ADDER_8BIT. Inputs a and b at 8 bits wide; cin at 1 bit.
  2. Use splitters to break a and b into their individual bits.
  3. Place eight instances of your FULL_ADDER subcircuit. Do not hand-wire forty gates.
  4. Bit 0 (least significant) takes the circuit's cin.
  5. Each stage's cout becomes the next stage's cin, running LSB → MSB.
  6. Bit 7's cout becomes the circuit's cout.
  7. Recombine the eight sum bits with a splitter into the 8-bit sum output.
  8. Set cin's pull behaviour to pull-down so it resets to 0. Left floating, the adder can come up holding a stale carry and give a wrong answer on your first test while every gate is wired correctly.
  9. Verify at minimum: 10000001 + 10000001 (which overflows into cout), one addition producing no carry at all, and one with cin = 1.

Circuit 4 — DECODER_2TO4

  1. New circuit tab named DECODER_2TO4. One 2-bit input pin named sel.
  2. Split sel into its two bits. Run each through a NOT gate so both polarities are available.
  3. Four 2-input AND gates, one per output, each wired to the combination that selects it: out0 = both inverted, out3 = both true.
  4. Verify: each of the four input values raises exactly one output and leaves the other three at 0.

Circuit 5 — DECODER_3TO8

  1. Same method, one input wider: three input bits, six polarities, eight 3-input AND gates.
  2. sel = 000 raises out0, through sel = 111 raising out7.
  3. Verify all eight input values.

Circuit 6 — DECODER_4TO16

  1. Same method again: four input bits, eight polarities, sixteen 4-input AND gates.
  2. sel = 0000 raises out0, through 1111 raising out15.
  3. Verify a representative sample across the range, including 0000 and 1111.
Build this one directly — do not cascade two 3×8 decoders

Wider decoders are built by extending the pattern, not by combining narrower ones. Cascading is a workaround from an era when decoders came as fixed-size chips and engineers combined what they could buy. It also requires an enable input, which your DECODER_3TO8 does not have.

Getting Help

  • Stuck on the concepts? Pages 1 and 2 of this week cover the adders; page 3 covers the decoders. The simulators on those pages let you set any inputs and watch the outputs, which is a fast way to check your own reasoning against a working model.
  • Stuck on the tool? Week 2 page 4 has the nine-minute Logisim walkthrough, and the official Logisim documentation is thorough.
  • Circuit gives wrong answers? Verify the smallest piece first. A broken FULL_ADDER becomes eight broken stages, and it is far easier to find at one gate than at forty.
  • Reference reading: combinational circuits are covered in Unit 3: Fundamentals of Digital Logic Design in Saylor CS301. Log into your CS301 course page and navigate to Unit 3.
