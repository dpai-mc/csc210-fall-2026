Week 9 — The Loop That Runs Everything

The stored-program machine and the fetch-decode-execute cycle  |  10/12 – 10/18  |  Estimated time: ~4.25 hours, plus finishing the midterm

For eight weeks you have built the parts of a computer. You have also been running them.

A register, an ALU, a RAM and its address register are in your file. But every byte that moved between them moved because you raised an s or an e line by hand. You have been the control unit.

This week opens Phase 3 by asking what a real processor does in your place: where it finds its instructions, and how it turns each one into a sequence of things the hardware does, one clock cycle at a time.

fetch → decode → execute → fetch → …

Which machine, and why

This week traces MIPS — the processor whose programs you have been writing in MARS — rather than the CPU you are building. It is a real design, it runs exactly the instructions you know, and every address and value on these pages matches what MARS shows you. Page 3 ends by setting it beside your CPU: the same loop, at a smaller size. Your CPU’s own instruction format comes later in the term.
