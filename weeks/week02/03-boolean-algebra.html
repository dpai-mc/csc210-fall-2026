Boolean Algebra

Estimated time: ~1.5 hours  |  Section: 2.4  |  Prerequisite: Page 2 (NAND and NOR)

What We Have / What We Can't Do / What We Can Now Do

What you already know: NAND is universal, and you have watched all three derivations work.

What you can't do yet: Prove it. The OR-from-NAND circuit works, but you took that on evidence rather than argument.

What you will be able to do by the end of this page: Manipulate Boolean expressions with the standard identities, apply De Morgan's laws, prove why the NAND derivations must work, and simplify a circuit before you ever build it.

Boolean algebra is the mathematics of logic gates. Just as ordinary algebra lets you simplify a mathematical expression, Boolean algebra lets you simplify a circuit. A simpler expression means fewer gates, which means faster, cheaper, more reliable hardware.

First: How to Read the Notation

Before any of the laws below will make sense, you need to read Boolean expressions correctly. This section takes two minutes and will save you hours.

Written asMeansAlso written
ABA AND BA · B, A * B, A && B
A + BA OR BA | B, A || B
A'NOT A¬A, !A, A with a bar over it

Two things trip people up every single semester.

The prime applies to ONE variable — read it like an exponent

In an expression like A'B, the prime applies only to the variable it immediately follows. Nothing else.

  • A'B = (NOT A) AND B
  • AB' = A AND (NOT B)
  • A'B' = (NOT A) AND (NOT B)

To negate a whole expression you must parenthesize it first: (AB)' means NOT (A AND B).

These look similar

A'B'
NOT A, ANDed with NOT B

(AB)'
NOT of (A AND B)

They are not the same

Try A = 1, B = 0.
A'B' = 0 · 1 = 0
(AB)' = (1·0)' = 0' = 1

The relationship between them is De Morgan's law, which is the heart of this page.

The second thing: + is OR, not addition. In Boolean algebra 1 + 1 = 1. If that looks wrong, you are reading it as arithmetic. There is only one bit of output; it is either on or it is not.

2.4 — The Laws

These are the tools. You do not need to memorize all of them today — you need to recognize them well enough to look them up and apply them. De Morgan's laws are the two you should know cold.

Identity

A + 0 = A
A · 1 = A

Null

A + 1 = 1
A · 0 = 0

Idempotent

A + A = A
A · A = A

Complement

A + A' = 1
A · A' = 0

Commutative

A + B = B + A
A · B = B · A

Associative

(A + B) + C = A + (B + C)
(A · B) · C = A · (B · C)

Distributive

A · (B + C) = AB + AC
A + (B · C) = (A + B)(A + C)

Double negation

(A')' = A

De Morgan's Laws

(A · B)' = A' + B'
(A + B)' = A' · B'

The second Distributive form — A + BC = (A + B)(A + C) — has no analogue in ordinary algebra and looks wrong the first several times you see it. Check it with a truth table in the widget below if you do not believe it.

De Morgan's Laws, and Why They Are the Point

In words:

  • (A · B)' = A' + B' — "the NOT of an AND is the OR of the NOTs"
  • (A + B)' = A' · B' — "the NOT of an OR is the AND of the NOTs"

Negating an expression does not just flip the answer. It flips the operator too. AND becomes OR; OR becomes AND.

This is why the NAND derivations work

On the last page you built OR from three NAND gates and confirmed it against the truth table. Here is the proof you were promised.

The first two NAND gates produce A' and B'. The third NAND computes:

NAND(A', B')  =  (A' · B')'

And by De Morgan's second law, (A' · B')' = (A')' + (B')' = A + B.

So the circuit computes OR — not by coincidence, but because De Morgan's law says a NAND fed with inverted inputs is an OR gate. The AND derivation is the same story: AB = ((AB)')', which is a NAND followed by an inverter.

De Morgan's laws are not simplification tricks. They are the mathematical foundation that makes NAND universality possible.

Simplification: Fewer Gates Is Faster Hardware

Consider this expression:

F = AB + AB'

Apply the distributive law, then the complement law, then identity:

F = A(B + B')      distributive
F = A(1)           complement:  B + B' = 1
F = A              identity:    A · 1 = A

An expression that appears to require an AND gate, a NOT gate, and an OR gate — three gates and their propagation delay — is a piece of wire. The B input is irrelevant. The output always equals A.

This is the practical payoff of Boolean algebra. Every gate you remove is a gate that does not have to be fabricated, powered, or waited on.
