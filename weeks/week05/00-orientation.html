Week 5 — Two Tracks, One Machine

The Register, and the Language  |  09/14 – 09/20  |  Estimated time: ~9.25 hours

Where you are, and what is missing

What you learned last week: how a circuit remembers. Two NAND gates wired back into each other hold a bit, and four NAND gates hold a bit you can control. MEM_1BIT — the cell your whole machine is made of. Memory is feedback, and there is no other mechanism.

What you can't do yet: anything useful with one bit. Real values are bytes — a number, a character, an address — and a byte is eight bits that have to be stored together, captured at the same moment, and read back as one thing.

And there is a second problem you have not met. Once several of these things exist, they all need to hand values to each other over the same wires. Eight cells shouting onto one wire at once is not communication.

What you will be able to do by the end of this week: store, hold and read back a whole byte, with one line that says store this and a second that says now speak. And — the other half of the week — read and write the language those bytes turn out to be.

Phase 2 opens, and the course changes shape

Weeks 1 to 4 were one ladder: representation, then gates, then computation, then memory. Each week stood on the one before it.

From here there are two tracks running side by side, and they stay that way until Week 8.

Hardware

You keep building the machine. This week: the 8-bit register, which is Logisim Checkpoint 1. Then the ALU, then RAM.

Assembly

You start programming the machine. This week: the MIPS instruction set, and your first program running in a simulator. Then arithmetic, control flow, and memory.

This is not two subjects. It is one subject from both ends.

The reason they are interleaved rather than taught one after the other: every instruction you write in MARS is tied to a circuit that will eventually execute it, and every circuit you build is tied to an instruction it enables.

This week you will see that directly. The thing MIPS calls $t0 is the circuit you build on Monday — not something like it, the same device, thirty-two cells wide instead of eight.

The two tracks converge for good in Week 8, when you build the RAM that the load and store instructions address.

What you build

  • MEM_8BIT Eight of last week's cells with one shared write line. The step from a bit to a byte, and it is the easiest thing you will do all semester — because you built the hard part last week.
  • ENABLE_8BIT The output stage. It is what lets a register stop driving a shared wire, which turns out to need a component that is not a logic gate. Week 6's ALU reuses this one.
  • REGISTER_8BIT Both of the above, plus two control pins and a monitor output. R0, R1, R2, R3, the program counter, the memory address register and the instruction register in your finished CPU are all copies of this circuit.

Three circuits, and the third one is the most reused component in the machine.

A full week, for the first time since Week 3

No holiday, no quiz

Week 4 was short — Labor Day removed a day and Quiz 1 took the rest. This week is back to the full load: about 9.25 hours, split roughly evenly between the two tracks.

Assignment 5 is 100 points and covers both tracks in one submission: the three circuits (that is Checkpoint 1, and 50 of the 100 points), three written questions, seven encoding and decoding problems, and a screenshot of your first MARS program.

Lab 5 is the first lab that does not finish in the room

Labs 1 through 4 all completed in class. Lab 5 (Tuesday) starts the register with you and you finish it on your own.

That is a change in how the labs work, not a warning about difficulty — but it does mean starting early enough to ask questions before the week ends.

Which components you may use — and one that catches people out

You may freely use anything from Logisim's Wiring folder (input and output pins, splitters, tunnels, constants, probes) and anything from Gates.

That includes the controlled buffer, and you need nine of them this week. It is in the Gates folder, it is permitted, and page 1 explains what it does and why it cannot be built out of AND, OR and NOT. After three weeks of "build it from gates" it is natural to hesitate over it — don't.

Nothing from Plexers, Arithmetic, or Memory. The Memory folder contains a Register that would do this week's circuits for you in about ten seconds, and produce a circuit that works and teaches nothing.
