Week 11 — The Control Unit

The clock, the stepper, and the circuit that turns an instruction’s bits into control lines  |  10/26 – 11/01  |  Estimated time: ~9 hours

You have been the control unit.

Last week you ran ADD R0, R1 on your own CPU core. To do it you raised regb_e, pulsed tmp_s, dropped regb_e, raised rega_e, pulsed acc_s and flags_s, and so on: a dozen pin changes, in an order you had to get exactly right, for one instruction. A real CPU does that hundreds of millions of times a second and never gets the order wrong.

This week you build the circuit that does it for you. A clock that shapes every transfer into four quarters, a stepper that counts the transfers of an instruction, and a control unit that reads an instruction byte and raises the right lines at the right step. By Sunday you will set one byte, click one input 24 times, and watch your CPU execute the instruction on its own.
