CPU Project Plan

10 points · complete/incomplete  |  Week 10  |  Due Sunday 10/25, 11:59 pm  |  Print to PDF and upload to Canvas

Your final project, due 12/11, is a working Logisim CPU. This plan is where you decide what yours will do, and check that your tests test it, while changing your mind is still cheap. It is worth 10 points, graded complete/incomplete: complete earns all 10. Complete means every section below is answered, and the test bytes you write decode to the instructions you say they are — the check my bytes buttons do that for you. You get written feedback either way.

Nothing you type on this page is saved anywhere

There is no storage behind these boxes. Close the tab or reload and your typing is gone. Print to PDF before you leave.

The three tiers

Graded by what your finished CPU executes, from RAM, correctly. Page 4 of Week 10, §10.16, explains them.

TierYour CPU executesInstructions
Cfetch, and every ALU instructionADD SHR SHL NOT AND OR XOR CMP — 8
Beverything in C, plus memory and immediate values+ LD ST DATA — 11
Aeverything in B, plus every jump and CLF, and runs MYSIM.txt to R2 = 25+ JMPR JMP Jcaez CLF — 15

At every tier you also submit one .circ file, a test program per class of instruction with expected results, and written documentation.

3. Test programs

For each class you ticked, write a short program as bytes in hex, one instruction per line, starting at address 00, and say what it should leave in the registers. Use DATA to set up values only if you are building tier B or A; at tier C, say what you will load into the registers by hand before running. The check button decodes your bytes so you can see whether they are what you meant.

3a. ALU test. Bytes, then the starting and expected register values.

3b. Memory and immediate test (tiers B and A; write “not building” for C).

3c. Jump test (tier A; write “not building” otherwise). A loop that runs a known number of times is a good test. MYSIM.txt is required at tier A in addition to this.
