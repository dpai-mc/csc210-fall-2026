2. (3 points) With opcode 111 selected on your assembled ALU, what appears on C, and why is that different from C showing 00000000?

3. (3 points) A branch offset is counted in instructions, not bytes. Explain what would be wasted if it counted bytes, and what effect that choice has on how far a branch can reach.

Part D — A MARS program (30 points)

Submit as lastname_firstname_week7.asm. This is a separate file upload — do not paste it into the PDF.

.data n: .word 10 fibn: .word 12 ga: .word 48 gb: .word 18 combo: .word 8

Four procedures

ProcedureTakes / returnsWhat it does
sum_1_to_n$a0 = N → $v01 + 2 + … + N, with a loop
fib$a0 = N → $v0the Nth Fibonacci number, iteratively. F₀ = 0, F₁ = 1
gcd$a0, $a1 → $v0greatest common divisor, by repeated subtraction
sum_and_fib$a0 = N → $v0sum_1_to_n(N) + fib(N) — it calls the other two

Call all four from the top level, in the order above, with n, fibn, ga/gb and combo, leaving the results in $s0, $s1, $s2, $s3. Then exit cleanly.

Three requirements that carry most of the marks

1. sum_and_fib needs a stack frame; the other three do not. It calls two procedures, so jal destroys its $ra, and it needs its argument again after the first call. The other three are leaves that use only $t registers — they need no prologue, no epilogue and no stack at all. Do not give them frames they do not need, and be ready to say why in one line of comment on each.

2. gcd's comparison must be a hand-written slt plus branch. Not blt, not bgt. Once in the program, written out, so the mechanism is something you have done. Pseudo-instructions are fine everywhere else.

3. No mult, div, mfhi, mflo or lui. Everything above can be done with addition and subtraction — which is also true of the ALU you just finished, and that is not a coincidence.

Program requirements

  • .data first, then .text. No main: label and no .globl main.
  • Leave MARS's delayed branching off (it is off by default).
  • A comment header with your name and a one-line description.
  • Each procedure preceded by a comment block: what it takes, what it returns, and whether it needs a frame and why.
  • A comment on every instruction saying what it does.

Optional bonus — sum_rec (up to +3)

Write a recursive version of sum_1_to_n: sum_rec(N) = N + sum_rec(N-1), with sum_rec(0) = 0. Call it with $a0 = 10 and leave the result in $s4; it should be 55, the same as $s0.

It must save $ra and $a0 in each call's own frame — page 4 §7.12 walks through exactly this shape with factorial.

This is genuinely optional. Recursion is taught this week but not required, and attempting this cannot lose you points — an incorrect sum_rec is simply worth zero bonus.
