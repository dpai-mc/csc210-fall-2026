Week 15 — How Fast, and Who Built It

Performance; pipelining; macros, assemblers, linkers, loaders and debuggers  |  11/23 – 11/29  |  Estimated time: ~4 hours

Your CPU multiplies 5 × 5 in 462 steps. Is that fast?

Last week your CPU became a finished machine: it loads a program, runs it, and stops. Every week before this one asked how to build something. This week asks two questions about what you built. How fast is it, compared with the processor in your laptop, and why? And who built the program: what happens between the text you type and the bytes that sit in memory?

The first question needs three numbers and one equation, and leads to the idea every modern processor is built around: doing several instructions at once. The second names tools you have used since Week 5 (the assembler and the debugger) and two you have not met by name (the linker, and the loader, which you built in Week 14).
